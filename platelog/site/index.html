The problem

The paperwork after the install is where money leaks out.

Registrations get missed

Many manufacturers give a longer parts warranty only if the unit is registered within a set window after install. Miss it, and the homeowner gets base coverage. When a compressor fails in year six, someone eats that bill.

Serial numbers live in tech phones

The photo gets taken, then lost in a camera roll. Three years later the customer calls and nobody knows what's in their attic without rolling a truck.

Tune-ups fall through the cracks

Maintenance customers are your steadiest revenue, and reminders depend on somebody remembering to call. Every missed spring check is a missed visit and a missed renewal.

Questions

What owners ask first

Do my techs need to install an app?

No. They open a link on their phone and take the photo. If they can text a picture, they can use PlateLog.

What if the scan reads a number wrong?

Every record is checked against the plate before it's saved, and low-confidence reads are flagged. Plates that are faded or covered in glare can be typed in by hand.

Does it register the warranty for me?

At launch it tracks every deadline, reminds you before it passes, and lines up the model and serial so registering takes a minute. Manufacturer portals vary, so a person still clicks submit.

Does it replace Housecall Pro or Jobber?

No. It runs next to whatever you use now, including paper. You can export your equipment list anytime.

Founding shops get early access first and help decide what ships. You're not charged until it's running for your shop.
